## Instructions

### 1. Brine the turkey (the day before)

1. Make sure the turkey is completely (or mostly) thawed. Remove the neck and giblets from the cavity. Save them to make stock for your gravy.
2. Dissolve the kosher salt and brown sugar in warm tap water, stirring until fully dissolved.
3. Add the rest of the 6 quarts as cold water, plus about 5 lb of ice. Stir, and refrigerate as needed until the brine is 40°F or below.
4. Place the turkey breast-side down in the brine, making sure it's completely submerged.
5. Cover and refrigerate for 12-24 hours. Keep it at 40°F or below the whole time.

### 2. Dry and prep the turkey

1. Remove the turkey from the brine and discard the brine.
2. Pat the turkey thoroughly dry with paper towels, including inside the cavity.
3. Set the turkey on a wire rack and let it air-dry, uncovered, in the refrigerator for 1-4 hours, or longer if you have time. This is what gets you crispy skin.
4. Before buttering, shape a triangular aluminum foil shield over the breasts. Remove it and set it aside to use during roasting if the breasts brown too quickly.

### 3. Make and apply the herb butter

1. Combine the softened butter, thyme, sage, black pepper, and garlic in a bowl. Mix until the herbs and garlic are evenly distributed.
2. With clean, food-safe gloved hands, gently separate the skin from the breast meat without tearing it.
3. Spread the herb butter generously under the skin, evenly across both breasts.
4. Spread the remaining butter over the outside of the turkey, including the legs and thighs.
5. Quarter the onion and apple and place them loosely in the cavity.
6. Tuck the wing tips under the turkey so they don't burn.

### 4. Roast

1. Preheat the oven to 350°F.
2. Place the turkey breast-side up on a roasting rack in a roasting pan.
3. Insert a remote thermometer probe into the thickest part of the breast, not touching bone.
4. Roast, watching the skin as it browns. If the breasts are browned enough before the turkey is done, cover them with the foil shield.
5. Start checking temperatures once the remote thermometer reads 155°F. Targets:
   * Breast: 155-160°F
   * Thigh: 170-175°F (165°F minimum)
   * Innermost part of the wing: 165°F
6. Confirm with an instant-read thermometer before taking the turkey out. Go by internal temperature, not time.

### 5. Rest and serve

1. Carefully move the turkey to a wire rack or a carving board with a drip tray.
2. Rest for 30-45 minutes before carving. Tent loosely only if needed (see notes).
3. While it rests, collect the pan drippings for gravy.
4. Remove the aromatics from the cavity, carve, and serve.

## Notes

* The brine and the foil breast shield come from Alton Brown.
* The recipe scales by the turkey's weight. Set it to your bird's weight and the brine, butter, and cook time all follow. Plan on about 1 lb of whole bird per person.
* Don't use a pre-brined, injected, or self-basting turkey. It can end up far too salty.
* The drier the skin is before the butter goes on, the crispier it gets.
* Don't cover the turkey tightly while it rests: trapped steam softens the skin. If you want to cover it, use a kitchen towel instead of foil, since the towel absorbs the moisture.
* Taste the pan drippings before adding any salt to the gravy.
